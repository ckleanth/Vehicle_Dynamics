Research project · vehicle dynamics · work in progress

A track car designed from the ground up

A track car designed from the ground up with vehicle dynamic response first. The concept is a road-legal track-day car featuring a steel space frame, benchmarked against the Caterham Seven SV - 150 mm wider - and engineered from the vehicle handling targets outward.

The simulation and validation method was proven on a real vehicle first: a multibody model of the designer's own modified Mazda MX-5 NC was simulated around Achna Speedway using logged racing lines, throttle, gear shifts, and braking inputs, then correlated directly against real-world telemetry (logged GPS, OBD data such as throttle, rpm, gears, braking, etc.). Custom tyre models were also developed in-house using physical testing and available tyre data.

The aim

A Lotus Seven class track car, designed from its handling targets

The project concept began several years ago with a brief for a space-frame car very close to the Caterham Seven SV, but a bit wider: road-legal, for track days, and designed vehicle dynamics first, with the yaw dynamic index and tyre models at its core and multibody software to check the concept. The handling targets ask for a car that is predictable, not snappy:

  • an understeer gradient of about 0.5 °/g, in a band of 0.4 to 0.6 °/g;
  • a yaw dynamic index DI = Izz/(m a b) of 0.89 to 0.91 as built;
  • a Seven-class unladen mass of at most 600 kg;
  • 15 in road tyres, with semi-slicks for track days, and one test track: Achna Speedway in Cyprus.

Before the multibody model is trusted, the method is proven on a real car. The designer's modified MX-5 NC is modelled in PyChrono, driven round Achna on his logged lap and checked against his data; its tyre calibration then carries over to the project concept tyres.

The proving car

The designer's MX-5 NC in Chrono::Vehicle

The test data

One logged lap of Achna Speedway

The logs come from RaceChrono: GPS, the phone's accelerometer and gyroscope, and the car's OBD channels (speed, rpm, throttle). The calibration lap is lap 5 of 30 May 2026: 84.04 s on a 1.91 km lap run clockwise, with 11 corners and speeds from 48 to 135 km/h.

  • The line is rebuilt from the gyroscope's curvature and the speed along the lap.
  • Corner 11 was drifted on purpose. There V·r, speed times yaw rate, reaches 1.70 g while the accelerometer reads 1.06 g: the car yawed faster than its path turned. The model's path takes that corner from the accelerometer and asks 1.10 g.
  • The GPS speed failed in a slow corner, falling to 21 km/h while the OBD speed, the rpm and the accelerometer showed 60 to 72 km/h. The dynamics read the OBD speed, scaled to the GPS on the fast parts.
  • Hot tyres. Lap 5 came while the tyres were fading: from lap 4 the lateral grip fell by 5 % and the traction by 9 %, so the calibrated grip is that of hot tyres.

Matching the test data

One grip factor holds the whole line

The model drives the designer's line on his throttle, gears and braking at eight grip scales, from 1.11 to 1.18. The calibrated grip is the lowest at which every corner stays within 1 m outside his line, with every higher grip doing the same: 1.14.

  • Corner by corner. The log's peak V·r is clearly higher only in corners 4 and 11: 1.10 and 1.70 g against the model's 0.97 and 1.01 g.
What the calibration meansλμ is the grip the designer's lap needed with this driver model and the assumed tyre. Where he drove below the limit it is a lower bound; the tyre's cornering stiffness, load sensitivity and slip at the peak are not measured.

Virtual tests

What the tests caught

Virtual tests check the model's parts before it is set against the logs. A slow ramp steer at 20 m/s gives an understeer gradient of 0.83 °/g with the LSD and 0.37 °/g with an open differential. The roll gradient is 3.16 °/g at 20 m/s and 3.12 °/g at 30 m/s, and the front takes 59 % of the elastic roll stiffness. With a second tyre model from another source, the model holds the line from a grip scale of 1.17, that tyre's own level at its best temperature (1.171), so the calibration carries over to the concept's tyres.

Each fault the tests found was fixed, and every calibration and test run again:

Anti-roll bars

Drop links at twice their distance

Found: the roll gradient rose with speed, from 3.18 °/g at 20 m/s to 4.28 °/g at 30 m/s. Chrono reads a bar's arm as one arm, from its centre to its drop link; the model had given it the whole span.

Fix: the drop links in place, the bar rates set anew.

Result: 3.16 and 3.12 °/g; the calibrated grip unchanged.

Solvers

Direct solvers locked the rear axle

Found: 1.625 °/g of understeer on Pardiso against 0.852 °/g on the default solver. The direct solvers held the driveline's differential-locking clutch rigid.

Fix: that clutch, which the model never engages, disabled.

Result: both solvers give 0.852 °/g.

Driveline

The propshaft torque rolled the body

Found: in a straight line the tyres' roll moment followed the propshaft torque, 0.994 for one: the simple engine model puts no reaction on the body.

Fix: the engine and gearbox's reaction applied at every step.

Result: −0.008 for one; the calibrated grip unchanged.

Differential and bars

The LSD never engaged

Found: every early run had an open differential, and the front took 72 % of the roll moment against an intended 61 %.

Fix: the LSD with Mazda's figures, and the bars set per axle by a static roll test.

Result: with the NC2 geometry, the line held in all 11 corners.

Driver model

Throttle that made power understeer

Found: on corner 4's exit the front slip reached 18.5°, where the real car never understeered.

Fix: the logged throttle, gears and clutch drive the car; the path follower only steers.

Result: 7.1° of front slip on the same exit.

Test data

GPS speed that failed

Found: for about 4 s the GPS speed fell to 21 km/h while the OBD speed, the rpm and the accelerometer showed 60 to 72 km/h.

Fix: the OBD speed, scaled to the GPS on the fast parts.

Result: the dynamics read the OBD speed in every session that has it.

The concept

Three designs and a decided suspension

Phase-1 models in Python set the concept: a mass and inertia build-up, Fiala and Magic Formula tyres, the bicycle model and its understeer budget, a two-degree-of-freedom yaw and sideslip model (step steer, steer reversal), the quarter car and the pitch plane for ride, and a quasi-steady lap. Making the car up to 150 mm wider than the Seven SV raises its grip by only 0.003 g, and on a synthetic test track the wider car gains 0.010 s a lap on road tyres; the designs analysed so far take the SV's track plus 50 mm, 1.496 m.

Three designs

  • A, the lead design: a Ford Duratec 2.0 at the concept's width.
  • B: a Ford Duratec 2.5 at A's width, with a close-ratio 6-speed gearbox.
  • C: a Ford Coyote 5.0 V8 car of the MX-5 NC's size, on 17 in wheels.

Every design has its gearbox behind the flywheel with a propshaft, and the fuel tank behind the driver's bulkhead, near the centre of mass. In load case T75, a 75 kg driver and half a tank:

  • Understeer and mass: A and B lie in the understeer band and within the Seven class; C is heavier and waits for its own suspension.
  • Balance: all three carry a little more on the front axle than the brief's assumed 47 to 50 %.
  • Dynamic index: all three fall short of the target as built. Dead weight at the rear, about 28 kg for A and B and 18 kg for C, brings each to 0.89; every 10 kg at the rear end adds 0.020.

The yaw dynamic index compares the car's yaw inertia with that of the same mass split between its two axles. At one, each axle responds to its own tyre forces without moving the other; below one, the car responds more quickly in yaw. NHTSA's measured vehicles give the context: 121 passenger cars from 0.91 to 1.18 (5th to 95th percentile) and 7 sports cars from 0.94 to 1.17. The concept's designs, with their mass between the axles, sit lower; no measured inertia of a Seven-type car was found.

The decided suspension

Double wishbones at the front and a de Dion axle at the rear located by an A-frame, as on the SV, with polyurethane wishbone bushes of 3.33 kN/mm (measured). Decided on 23 November 2026:

  • the steering rack ahead of the axle, and a rear anti-roll bar;
  • rear roll steer −0.095 deg/deg and rear anti-squat 28 %;
  • the front bar on 200 mm levers, and nearly parallel steering;
  • in load case T75: ride frequencies of 1.81 Hz front and 2.00 Hz rear on the springs, a roll gradient of 0.82 °/g, and 56 % of the lateral load transfer at the front.

The understeer budget adds Gillespie's terms: the tyres, camber, roll steer, compliance steer, aligning torque, load transfer and the steering's compliance. It totals 0.494 °/g in load case T75, and 0.494 to 0.507 °/g over the four load cases.

Dampers

Two stages per direction, at the damping levels of the MX-5's Öhlins: a damping ratio of 0.60 below a knee of 0.20 m/s of damper speed and 0.35 above it, on the spring's wheel rate, for every design of the concept and the MX-5 model. Rebound is 1.37 times bump, the mean of Milliken's racing figures. At the dampers, bump / rebound below the knee: 2,752 / 3,771 N s/m at the front and 1,932 / 2,646 N s/m at the rear; above it 1,606 / 2,200 and 1,127 / 1,544 N s/m.

Limits

  • The MX-5's suspension geometry is a best estimate, not measured; the front coil-over's lever ratio, 0.78 from the literature, and the 30 mm ride lowering stay assumed until the ride heights are measured.
  • The tyre is an assumed TMeasy set with one grip factor; its cornering stiffness is not measured.
  • Not modelled: ABS and EBD, and Achna's elevation and camber.
  • The driver model steers a fixed line; it does not drift as the logged lap did in corner 11.
  • The concept's figures rest on a mass build-up assumed item by item and on design values: they are not tests of a built car.
